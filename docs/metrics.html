Metric library

The complete set of metrics FantasyLabs documents publicly in its support glossaries — with the official definition, our engine function, and the source. This is the “data quality” contract; everything else builds on it.

How to read this “Definition” paraphrases the official glossary (quotes are short and attributed). “Engine” is the original function in engine/fantasylab_engine/metrics.py. Conflicting official definitions are flagged ⚠ (I4/I5/I6).

Implied Points Points a player is expected to score historically given his cost. implied_points_from_expectation(expected) ← ExpectationModel.expected(salary) S11

Upside / Breakout ⚠ I5 NBA + MLB glossaries: % of games ≥ ½ SD above salary-based expectation. NFL: worded as “highest Plus/Minus scores” (vague — NFL is the outlier). upside_rate_half_sd (default, 2:1 official majority) · upside_rate_highest_pm (NFL variant) S12 S13 vs S11
Duds ⚠ I4 NBA + MLB glossaries: % of games ≥ ½ SD below expectation. NFL: % of games scoring < ½ of salary-based expectation — two different formulas; NFL is the outlier. dud_rate_half_sd (default) · dud_rate_half_expectation (NFL variant) S12 S13 vs S11

Ceiling/Floor (NBA) ⚠ I6 Same 15%/70%/15% band, but from a volatility rating built on usage, on-off, and shot charts (own + opponent). simulator percentiles (SimLineupResult.p95/p05 at lineup level; player-level via draws) S12

The engine of it all: salary → expectation

The glossaries are explicit that expectations come from “a database of historic salaries and fantasy performances on each daily fantasy site” rather than a fragile $/point ratio or arbitrary tiers (S11). Their regression parameters are not public (flag I15). Our ExpectationModel is an original monotone binned fit with documented fallbacks — see Methodology.
